# Research outputs register

What came out of this thesis research: findings, upstream reports and contributions, reusable methods and tools,
and lessons. One row for each output, with its status and the evidence behind it. The research journal
(`RESEARCH_JOURNAL.md`) stays the source of truth; this file is the index of results that other people can use.

Started 2026-10-29. Update it when an output changes status (for example, a bug report is filed or a fix is merged).

Status words: VALIDATED (passed its preregistered rule and an independent review), CONFIRMED WITH LIMITS (a review
confirmed it with stated scope limits), PENDING VALIDATION, DRAFT, FILED (posted upstream), NEGATIVE (a clear "no").

| O-07 | Exact model symmetries (permuting hidden units, heads or the residual basis, sign flips, power-of-two scales) cannot make the operands a tensor-core lane sees much more alike: 0.5% fewer bit transitions (median), 2.5% at best, against 72% to 82% for a non-exact per-row sort. | O-10 | On 1 x H100 (vLLM nightly, FA4), Gemma 4 31B FP8 at batch 4 stores the same K=V data twice in its global layers, and the global attention reads it at the copy bandwidth. A compact cache could save at most 6.7% of decode time at 32K context and 11.3% at 64K (one-sided 95% bounds 6.5% to 6.8% and 11.1% to 11.5%). This is a ceiling, not a measured speedup. The sliding layers stay flat. For 26B-A4B at batch 8 the 32K ceiling is 6.1% (bounds 4.6% to 7.6%); 64K is not known because of a probe fault. | CONFIRMED WITH LIMITS (R-65) | Gemma 4 31B FP8 and 26B-A4B BF16; 1 x H100; vLLM 0.33.0rc1.dev229+g3966924d5; batch 4 and 8; BF16 KV; text only; one GPU, one session | E-49 (`experiments/gemma4_kv_audit/`); R-64, R-65; D-74, D-75 |
| O-11 | On 1 x H100 (vLLM nightly), Gemma 4 31B FP8 with the stock FP8 KV cache at about 82% of the KV pool: a compact K=V cache could still save at most 12.1% of decode time at 32K context (batch 19) and 15.0% at 64K (batch 11); the lower bounds are 12.0% and 14.9%. With the BF16 cache near its pool limit, the ceiling is 13.7% and 14.5%. This is a ceiling, not a measured speedup: it assumes that time follows bytes, and the stock FP8 kernel has a time elasticity to bytes of only 0.49, so the real saving is plausibly 4% to 8% (R-68). A compact kernel must realize 41% (32K) or 33% (64K) of the byte saving to keep 5%. | CONFIRMED WITH LIMITS (R-68) | Gemma 4 31B FP8; 1 x H100; vLLM 0.33.0rc1.dev229+g3966924d5 (the FP8 dequant kernel on the global layers, FA3 on the sliding layers); text only; decode only; one GPU, one session | E-50 (`experiments/gemma4_kv_headroom/`); R-67; D-81, D-82 |
| O-12 | On the same setup at batch 4, the stock FP8 KV path for Gemma 4 31B is slower than the BF16 path below about 40K context (5.8% slower at 2K, 1.5% at 32K) and only 2.6% faster at 64K. Its global attention reads at about 0.69 x the copy bandwidth in FP8 bytes, so the time per context token falls by about a quarter, not by half. At a larger batch the crossing moves down: at B = 11 (FP8) against B = 10 (BF16), FP8 is 5% faster per step at 32K and 11% slower at 2K (R-68). FP8 KV also holds about 2 times the requests at 32K. | CONFIRMED WITH LIMITS (R-68) | As O-11; batch 4; two stock paths (FA3 plus FA4 FP8-dequant against FA4 for all layers) | E-50 m31f8 and m31bf cells at B = 4 |
| O-13 | On 1 x H100 at batch 11 to 19 and 32K to 64K context, halving the DRAM bytes of the Gemma 4 31B global-layer FP8 decode attention (the K = V oracle) cuts its context-growing time by only 8% to 10%, for the stock FA4 FP8 dequant kernel and for a Triton FP8 kernel (K = V elasticity 0.17 to 0.20; 0.9 to 1.0 on an RTX 4090). INTERPRETATION (R-70): bytes explain at most about 20% of that time; the rest is on chip (candidates: the FP8 cast, the MMA with M padded from 8 to 64, the one-stage staging chain of FA4). The oracle measures DRAM bytes only, not the cast and staging savings of a real compact layout. A simple Triton split-KV FP8 kernel was 8% to 12% faster on that time than the stock kernel. A Triton compact K=V FP8 kernel was 29% slower than its standard twin, from an FP8-only defect of the implementation (its BF16 version is faster at twice the bytes); the BF16 Triton pair is bound by bytes, and the compact layout cuts 34% to 37% | EXPLORATORY (E-51 was INVALID by its frozen rule: V3 at one 2K cell, V4 spread; R-70 CONFIRM_WITH_CORRECTIONS: every number recomputes). E-52 (2026-11-01, also INVALID by V4 under the power cap) agrees: with FP16 compute, K = V elasticity 0.22 to 0.24, and the pool rows (all bytes from L2) cut the time by only 14% to 22% in all three FP8 kernel families, so about 80% of the time is on-chip work; the views layout gives 4% to 7% (Triton) and 7% to 10% (stock) | One H100, one session, the FA4 dequant kernel of vLLM 3966924d5 and the author's Triton kernels; the byte route only; not the FP8 MMA kernel of FA PR 199, and not a competent compact FP8 kernel | `experiments/gemma4_kv_kernel/README.md` Section 13; `dossier/REVIEW_E-51_POSTRUN_2026-11-01.md` |
| O-14 | The compact K=V cache of the Gemma 4 global layers, stored in the row order [K_rot | V_unrot | V_rot] (640 values), is read by the unchanged stock vLLM FA4 kernel through two overlapping strided views (K = values 0 to 511 with the k_norm weight in the query, V = values 128 to 639, output order folded into o_proj): exact (the same error as the standard layout, 0.00166) and 7% to 10% faster on the context-growing attention time on one H100 (FP8 KV, B 11 to 19, 32K to 64K; BF16 KV: the Triton pair 30% to 33%). So the 37.5% memory saving needs no new attention kernel and costs no speed | EXPLORATORY (E-52 was INVALID by V4; R-72 pending) | One H100, one session; the FA4 FP8 dequant kernel of vLLM 3966924d5 and the BF16 FA4 path; microbenchmark, no engine; the cache write, the query prep and the o_proj fold are not built yet | `experiments/gemma4_kv_views/README.md` Section 13 |
| O-15 | For the Gemma 4 31B global layers on one H100 (FP8 KV, head_dim 512), a simple Triton split-KV decode kernel with FP16 compute is 24% to 25% faster on the context-growing attention time than vLLM's FA4 FP8 dequant kernel (2 split settings against a 54-configuration Triton sweep). ## 4. Lessons (operations and method)

| ID | Lesson | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| L-01 | Validate every log parser on a real log of the exact engine version; a new format silently hid an alias | E-40 (cell D V0) |
| L-02 | Cold compile caches change greedy output; warm every configuration before measuring | E-36 rehearsal (amendment A3) |
| L-03 | Time every per-item loop at the real data size before booking a GPU | E-38 first run ($14 lost) |
| L-04 | An idle GPU before the session starts is the largest cost risk; use a boot watchdog and do create-to-start in one step | E-42 incident (about $16 idle) |
| L-05 | A standalone kernel benchmark can miss the in-model cost; profile in the model | E-44, R-57 |
| L-06 | Check where a setting acts before measuring it; a config flag cannot reach inside an opaque op | E-45 cancelled by R-58 |
| L-07 | Before a novelty search on a mechanism, check that its host code path has a future (open RFCs, deprecation banners); read whole design docs, not only the lines a search matches | I-173 / R-61 (vLLM IR deprecated; compiler fusions moving to model code) |
| L-08 | Before a symmetry idea, compare the freedom it has (one choice per dimension) with the freedom the effect needs (one choice per element); a free CPU screen settled I-174 before any GPU spend | I-174 / E-47 / R-62 |
| L-09 | When a profile shows a clear optimization, search the target engine's open PRs by the mechanism's plain name in pass 1; real waste attracts other engineers fast (vLLM PR #60566 had the same idea three weeks earlier) | I-181 / E-48 / R-63 |
| L-10 | Before a profiling test on a new model type, count the engine's open PRs per code path (vLLM: 96 for GDN, 39 for Gemma) and read the model's own design report next to the engine's model code. A report can state a saving that no engine implements (Gemma 4: K=V gives 37.5% less global KV; vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM store full K and V) | D-73 pass 1 / I-182 / E-49 |
| L-11 | In a decode-only timing probe, set `max_tokens` from the prefill schedule, not from the timed steps only. With chunked prefill, early requests decode while later ones prefill: in a Gemma 4 26B-A4B 64K cell at batch 8, request 0 used 226 of its 236 tokens before the timing started, and one request ended in each block. Check the running batch after the timed blocks and log each request's finish reason. `min_tokens` cannot stop a length cap (corrected by R-65; the earlier advice `min_tokens` = `max_tokens` is retracted in journal Section 8) | E-49 m26 64K cells; R-65 |
| L-12 | A profile window is not a timing window: torch.profiler start and stop made the step 1.6 to 2.4 times longer than the unprofiled median on the H100 (2.0 times on the RTX 4090). Take wall time from unprofiled blocks and only device time from the profile | R-64 R1; E-49 rehearsal and H100 run |
| L-13 | The boot watchdog starts with the container, after the image pull. A cold host took 11 min to pull the vLLM image, so count the pull in the cost cap. A log watcher must match the final line exactly ("setup done after" also contains "done after") and must not count a log that does not exist yet as an ssh failure | E-50 H100 session |
| L-14 | Before reading a byte-proportional ceiling as a speedup, measure how the kernel's time follows its bytes (E-50: elasticity 0.49 from BF16 to FP8 KV). A batch-4 calibration can also hide a split-choice dip at a larger batch (the BF16 head-dim-512 kernel used about 80 of 132 SMs at B = 5 and 10) | E-50; R-68 |
| L-15 | In a kernel sweep, the best row per cell is one biased measurement, and the best rows at two context lengths can come from two configurations. Select one configuration per family on the sweep, time it again in 5 interleaved repeats (`MEASUREMENT.md` Section 1.5) and check that the decision replicates. Check every swept configuration for correctness, because a wrong kernel is often fast. A KILL by a twin kernel needs a competent twin (E-51: the compact kernel at 0.6 x copy) | E-51 design; R-69 R1, R2, R5, O3 |
| L-16 | A short-context baseline row of a memory-bound kernel can sit in L2 on a GPU with a large L2 (RTX 4090: 72 MB; 57.7 MB of compact FP8 rows read at 1.15 x copy). A bandwidth positive control must use rows much larger than L2 or the growth rate between two lengths, and a cold first timing needs an untimed warm-up pass | E-51 S5 rehearsal; A2 |
| L-17 | On an H100 SXM, heavy decode attention kernels ran under the software power cap (SM clock 1,980 to 1,530 MHz, up to 701 W), and CUDA-graph replay spreads reached 4.2% on a deciding row (17% at most) against 0.8% on an RTX 4090. Size the spread check and the replays for this noise, and tie a microbenchmark to the engine on the growth (dt), not on a short-context time: the fixed cost differs (graph padding, other kernels). A throttle check that counts only the hard reasons (0x8, 0x20, 0x40, 0x80) misses the software power cap (0x4: 14.6% of the busy samples in E-51). Count the cap as its own line and log the SM clock per replay set with NVML (R-70) | E-51 S6; R-70 |
| L-18 | A rehearsal on a GPU with much less DRAM bandwidth cannot show an on-chip defect. On the RTX 4090 (0.92 TB/s) the compact FP8 kernel passed its gate at 1.01 and 0.99 x copy; on the H100 (3.0 TB/s) it read 0.37 x copy. A gate against the twin on DRAM-bound rows also passes there, because the bytes hide the on-chip cost. Add a rehearsal gate on an L2-resident shape, where DRAM does not set the time: there the compact kernel must not be slower than its twin | E-51 S5 and S6; R-70 |
| L-19 | On an H100 SXM under the software power cap, a short row that follows heavy rows can run at a lower clock in some repeats (E-52: one 2K row at 1,515 to 1,715 MHz in 3 of 5 repeats, a 5.2% replay spread), while dt moves 0.6% at most. A replay-spread bound on short rows voided E-51 and E-52 although their deciding numbers were far from the lines. Bound dt across repeats instead, report the replay spread and the NVML clock per row, and add a cool-down before short rows | E-51, E-52; R-70, R-71 |
