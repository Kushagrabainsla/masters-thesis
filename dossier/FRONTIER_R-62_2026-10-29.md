## 0. Method: the cost equation
Cost per useful token or step = (capex / useful life + power + other opex) / useful work per hour.
Useful work per hour = peak rate x utilization x goodput fraction. So every frontier concern is one term:

| Term | Frontier concern | Physical limit | What we can monitor on a rented GPU (no root) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Sites are power-limited; tokens per watt | GPU power limit (H100 700 W); data-dependent switching | NVML power, clocks, throttle flags, temperature |
| Utilization | MFU in training, bandwidth use in decode | Roofline: FLOP/s, HBM bytes/s | Kernel timeline (torch.profiler, nsys), timing |
| Memory bandwidth | Decode tokens per second | HBM bytes per token | Bytes moved / time; gather microbenchmarks |
| Goodput | Failures, stragglers, preemption | Mean time to failure x job size | Not at scale (needs a fleet) |
| Useful life | Depreciation, wear-out, failure rate | Temperature, voltage, thermal cycles | Temperature and power only; no lifetime ground truth |
| Elastic capacity | Spot and preemptible GPUs | Notice time, state size, restart time | Single-node restart cost only |

Constraints that shape the choice: about 9 weeks full time; rented Runpod Pods without root (no power limit or clock
control, no MIG, Nsight Compute counters may be blocked); one to eight GPUs; a Mac M4.

# 1. What changed in the world (numbers and sources)
- FACT (secondary sources; verify before citing): power, not GPU supply, now limits AI data-center growth. Grid
  connection takes about 2 to 7 years against 12 to 18 months to build a site; the US interconnection queue is about
  2,600 GW (Futurum); Stargate Abilene is quoted at 1.2 GW peak.
- FACT: synchronous training makes large, fast power swings (compute phases against communication phases).
  Microsoft, OpenAI and NVIDIA (arXiv 2508.14318, 2025) study software injection of secondary work, GPU power
  smoothing (a floor at 90% of TDP costs about 10.5% energy in one simulation) and energy storage. GB300 adds power
  smoothing and storage in the power supply. In 2026 at least five more papers appeared (2607.19638 phase locking
  behind a shared cap; 2610.36441 model-predictive staggering, 74% less fluctuation in the 0.2 to 2 Hz band;
  2609.11542 job power elasticity; 2608.01250 inference power ramps; 2512.08076 battery plus supercapacitor).
- FACT (our runs): in vLLM serving on 1 x H100 (Qwen3-8B-FP8, batch 1 to 256), 46% (E-46) and 43% (E-44) of busy
  1-second NVML samples had the power-cap throttle flag. Median SM clock when capped: 1875 MHz (E-46) and 1950 MHz
  (E-44); minimum 1440 MHz; uncapped 1980 MHz. Median power 649 W capped, 482 W busy and uncapped.
- FACT: GEMM power depends on the data. Input patterns change GEMM power by up to about 38% (Gregersen, Patel,
  Choukse, arXiv 2409.18324, synthetic matrices only). On an A100 at its 330 W limit, an 8192^3 matmul ran at 295
  TFLOPS with zeros and 257 TFLOPS with randn (Horace He, 2024).
- FACT: reliability at scale. Llama 3 405B: 466 interruptions in 54 days on 16,384 H100s, 419 unexpected; GPU
  issues were the largest class (faulty GPU 148, HBM3 72); effective training time above 90% (Meta, via press
  summaries of the Llama 3 paper). H100 has about 3.2x lower per-GPU mean time between uncorrectable memory errors
  than A100 (arXiv 2503.11901); the authors estimate about 5% overprovisioning for GPU failures.
- FACT (analyst commentary, not filings): hyperscalers depreciate servers over 5 to 6 years (Google 6, Microsoft up
  to 6, Meta 5.5; Amazon cut some to 5 citing AI). Critics argue the economic life of AI GPUs is 2 to 3 years.
- FACT: utilization. MegaScale reached 55.2% MFU on 12,288 GPUs (arXiv 2402.15627). Batch-1 decode on H100 reached
  only 26.9% of peak HBM bandwidth in one 2026 study (arXiv 2605.30571), attributed to fixed per-step overhead.
- FACT: fail-slow and grey GPUs are an industry topic (GUARD, MLSys 2026 industry track; ByteDance FALCON). Thermal
  imbalance between GPUs couples with overlapped compute and communication (Lit Silicon, arXiv 2511.09861, MI300X,
  up to 6% performance).
- FACT: preemptible training keeps moving (Desai et al. 2026: preemption-rate prediction plus checkpoint-interval
  tuning, up to 60% more throughput; mixed spot and on-demand, 2.04x throughput at 51% lower cost).

# 2. Candidate non-consensus truths (10)

| # | Area | Conventional assumption | Possible truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Power | A model's power is set by its shapes and kernels | Exact model symmetries (permutation, sign, power-of-two scale) can reshape bits to cut switching power at no accuracy cost |
| C2 | Power | Training power swings need hardware or storage fixes | Useful co-located work can fill the troughs |
| C3 | Power | Serving throughput is set by the scheduler, not by power | Power-aware batching can avoid power-cap throttling |
| C4 | Utilization | CUDA graphs remove decode overhead | Small-batch decode still wastes most HBM bandwidth |
| C5 | Bandwidth | Paged KV gathers run at full bandwidth | With 192 to 288 GB pools, scattered KV blocks exceed GPU TLB reach |
| C6 | Utilization | Autotuned kernels are the fastest in production | Short, random-data autotuning mis-ranks kernels under a sustained power cap with real data |
| C7 | Longevity | GPU life is fixed by the part | Temperature and thermal cycles set wear, and placement can level it |
| C8 | Reliability | A GPU either works or fails | Grey, slow GPUs silently slow synchronous jobs |
| C9 | Preemption | Spot training needs better checkpointing | (as stated) |
| C10 | Bandwidth | KV offload is limited by PCIe | Blackwell's hardware decompression engine can raise effective offload bandwidth |

# 3. Ideas killed by prior work or by our own screen
- C1 (I-174) KILL, exact tier, by our free screen (`experiments/power_shaping_screen/`). Exact moves cut the bit
  transitions a tensor-core lane sees by 0.5% (median) and 2.5% (best type); the non-exact sorted bound cuts 72% to
  82%. One permutation must serve all rows of a matrix, so it cannot make near-random rows alike. Tier b
  (power-aware rounding, a PowerPruning transfer) changes the model; WEAK, not recommended.
- C2 KILL: 2508.14318 (secondary-workload injection), GB300 smoothing, and five 2026 papers (Section 1).
- C3 KILL: small oracle (our telemetry: median clock loss when capped is about 5%, and only in part of the time) and
  prior work on power-aware serving (DynamoLLM 2408.00741, POLCA, TAPAS, 2608.01250). Clock control needs root.
- C4 KILL: measured already (2605.30571) and addressed by megakernels (Hazy, Mirage MPK; I-163), CUDA graphs and
  programmatic dependent launch.
- C7 KILL for this thesis: no lifetime ground truth within 9 weeks or without fleet data; only patents and older
  embedded thermal-scheduling work found. Temperature is measurable, life is not.
- C8 KILL: GUARD (MLSys 2026), FALCON, SuperBench, Lit Silicon.
- C9 KILL: Desai et al. 2026, Bamboo, Oobleck, Parcae, torchft, just-in-time checkpointing (registry I-93, I-100).
- C10 KILL by size: lossless LZ4, Snappy or Deflate gain little on FP8 or BF16 data (roughly 1.0x to 1.3x), and KV
  offload is crowded (LMCache, Mooncake; registry I-63, I-148).
- Kept for pass 2: C5 (I-175) and C6 (I-176), both MEASURE-FIRST with low priors.

# 4. Surviving ideas
None. Pass 1 kept C5 (I-175) and C6 (I-176) as low-prior MEASURE-FIRST items; pass 2 killed both (Section 9). The
strongest pass-1 lead, C1 (I-174), died in the free screen before any GPU spend. For the record, the two that lasted
longest:

**I-174 Data-aware power shaping (KILL, exact tier by screen; tier b by size)**
- Secret: GEMM power depends on operand bits; exact model symmetries might reshape the bits for free.
- Evidence: up to about 38% GEMM power swing between synthetic inputs (2409.18324); 15% throughput between zeros and
  randn at the A100 power cap; our H100 runs are capped 43% to 46% of busy seconds.
- Closest prior work: 2409.18324 names "sort the model weights through permutation invariance" as its own future
  work (not built); READ 2308.15698 and bus-invert coding 2304.12691 (ASIC); PowerPruning 2303.13997 (retrains).
- What killed it: one permutation (or sign, or scale) must serve every row, so for near-random rows the gain is the
  extreme-value limit, about 3.5 / sqrt(bits) (pass 2). Screen: 0.5% median, 2.5% best (Section 3). Even at 5% fewer
  transitions, the power-capped speed gain is under 1%. Tier b (accuracy-neutral rounding) moves about one mantissa
  bit: under 1%.
- Reviewer sentence: "The 38% swing needs per-element sorting; function-preserving symmetries cut toggles by 1 to 5%."

**I-175 TLB reach for large paged KV pools (KILL)**
- What killed it: 2 MiB pages and multi-GiB L2 TLB reach (Volta: about 8 GiB) already cover per-layer KV buffers
  (1 to 5 GB); scattered blocks already miss the L1 TLB, and memory parallelism hides the miss. vAttention
  (2405.04437) gives per-request virtual contiguity; LServe (2502.14866) studies page size. No Hopper or Blackwell TLB
  numbers are public, so a 1-hour probe is the most it could justify.

**I-176 Sustained-power kernel selection (KILL)**
- What killed it: at a fixed power cap, throughput is energy efficiency times the cap, so the capped ranking is the
  energy ranking, which tuners already study (Kernel Tuner 2211.07260, 2411.16954, the CUTLASS measurement guide,
  TensorRT-LLM PR 8779). Our telemetry bounds the gain: removing the cap completely gains at most 0.65% (E-44) to
  2.4% (E-46) of average throughput in this serving regime.

# 6. Best idea and the honest verdict
No defensible new thesis gap survives in these frontier problem areas under our constraints (9 weeks, no root,
1 to 8 rented GPUs). The areas are real and important, but each one is either crowded by 2025 to 2026 work, too small
in our measured regime, or not testable without fleet data. INTERPRETATION: this is the fifth broad pass (R-17, R-33,
R-36, R-54, R-62) with the same result. The one search method that has produced real findings in this project is
measuring real systems on GPUs (E-36 profiles gave O-03 and O-04), not literature or first-principles mining alone.
Both reviewers say that D-70 options A and B remain the best use of the time.

# 7. Exact first experiment
None is recommended for a new idea. The free screen (`experiments/power_shaping_screen/`, E-47) was the decisive
experiment for the strongest lead and it gave KILL. If the user still wants a new lead, the evidence-based next step
