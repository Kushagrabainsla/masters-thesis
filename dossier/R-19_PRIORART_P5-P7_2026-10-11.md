# R-19: Prior-art check for assumptions P5, P6, P7 (2026-10-11)

Method: remove an assumption. For each assumption: evidence, what breaks, prior art, remaining gap, verdict.
Labels: FACT = I opened the primary source. SNIPPET = seen only in a search result or a secondary page. INTERPRETATION = my own inference.
Search effort: at least 4 phrasings per assumption (about 8 for P5, 9 for P6, 8 for P7). Some arXiv IDs are in the 2606 to 2609 range. I opened each ID I cite as FACT.

## P5. "A human reads the output as it streams, so serve for TTFT and TPOT."

### Evidence the assumption is breaking
- FACT: TraceLab (UW SyFi blog, 2026-06-25) states "88% of all LLM rounds respond to a tool result rather than a human." https://syfi.cs.washington.edu/blog/2026-06-25-tracelab/
- FACT: In the same trace, a round decodes about 200 tokens on a 32k to 256k prefix. Inputs outnumber outputs 294 to 1 (52.56 B cached + 2.34 B new input tokens, 186.9 M output tokens).
- FACT: A tool call takes about 18 s on average. An LLM generation takes about 13 s. Tool calls over 1 min are 4% of calls and 85% of tool time.
- SNIPPET: NVIDIA AIPerf documents the corpus as 665,453 LLM rounds in 8,058 sessions from 52 developers.

### What breaks
- FACT: "Human-Less LLM Serving" (arXiv 2606.20577, Lian et al., cs.NI) measures the throughput cost of TTFT/TPOT SLOs on agent traffic. Setup: Qwen2.5-32B FP16, 8x H20, SGLang and Sarathi-Serve, Mooncake ToolAgent trace, closed loop.
  - At 64K context, tight chunking (C=512) gives 2050 tok/s against 5130 tok/s with no cap. That is a 60% gap. At 8K the gap is 15%. At 32K it is 40%.
  - At the production setting (C=8192) and 64K context, the agent workload loses 20.4% (5130 to 4260 tok/s). The Poisson human workload loses 1.9%.
  - The abstract says "60-93%". The body shows 15 to 60% in the sweep. I could not match 93% to a table. The paper also swaps the direction of the numbers between Sections 4.1 and 4.5. Treat all figures as unverified until the PDF tables are checked.
- FACT: The prototype HlServe (a patch on SGLang) gains only +5.3% over SGLang and about +1% over Sarathi-Serve.
- FACT: The paper does not study streaming versus non-streaming. It does not study diffusion or parallel decoding. Draft-model speculation ran out of memory. N-gram speculation had low acceptance.
- SNIPPET: SimpleTool (arXiv 2603.00030) reports 3 to 6x end-to-end speedup (up to 9.6x) for function calls by parallel decoding of name and arguments. It needs a trained model.
- SNIPPET: A Feb 2026 paper (arXiv 2602.07451) says diffusion-LM agents are on average over 30% faster end to end, with some cases above 8x. An ACL 2026 paper says diffusion LMs fail strict JSON schemas.
- INTERPRETATION: The cost of the human tax is real but depends on context length and on load. At short context it is small.

|---|---|---|

No paper found that proposes the term "time to last token" as the objective. No paper found that drops streaming at the API level to enable a different decode mode.

### Remaining gap
Existing work removes the SLO tax at the scheduler level, but no one lets the client declare "machine consumer, whole output" so the engine can also switch decode mode (parallel, jump-forward, grammar-forced spans) and quantify what is gained.

### Verdict: PARTIAL (the scheduling half is CROWDED; the decode-mode half is OPEN)

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB, public data (feasible):
  - Measure the fraction of output tokens in public tool-call benchmarks (for example BFCL) that a JSON schema forces. Use llama.cpp grammar with a 1 to 3B model. This bounds the possible jump-forward saving per tool call.
  - TraceLab has no text (FACT), so use it only for round counts and token sizes.
- One AWS H100, under $20 (feasible, about 3 to 5 GPU hours):
  - Replay a TraceLab-shaped closed loop (about 64K prefix, about 200 output tokens) on vLLM or SGLang with an 8B or 32B FP8 model. Sweep chunked-prefill size and batch cap.
  - Measure job completion time and tok/s against the Human-Less numbers. This also checks their 60% claim on one GPU.
  - Kill criterion: if the gap at 64K is under 10%, drop the throughput angle.

## P6. "The infrastructure operator and compute provider are trusted."

### Evidence the assumption is breaking
- SNIPPET: Apple Private Cloud Compute now runs outside Apple data centers, on Google Cloud, using NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs with Confidential Computing, Intel TDX and Google Titan. NVIDIA blog, WWDC 2026 coverage (DCD, InfoQ July 2026). Google also announced Confidential G4 VMs (RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell) in preview.
- Not found: a distinct "Google Private AI Compute" product page, or confidential-inference offerings from Anthropic or OpenAI. I did not open these.
- SNIPPET: Tinfoil (June 2026) publishes Blackwell CC benchmarks.
- SNIPPET: Decentralized markets mostly use reputation. io.net reportedly caught a Sybil cluster. Gensyn targets verifiable training. Prime Intellect published TOPLOC but I found no proof of production use.

### What breaks (numbers)
- FACT: PipeLLM (arXiv 2411.03357, ASPLOS 2025). Encryption costs up to 52.8% (OPT-30B) and 88.2% (OPT-66B) of throughput on H100 when models or KV swap. PipeLLM cuts overhead to under 19.6%.
- FACT: arXiv 2505.16501 (May 2025, single H100). With model swapping, confidential mode loses 45 to 70% throughput and has 20 to 30% higher latency.
- FACT: "The Serialized Bridge" (arXiv 2606.23969, Jun/Jul 2026). Blackwell (RTX Pro 6000, B300) under TDX plus GPU-CC. Throughput loss 13 to 27%. KV-restore penalty +131%. Model load 34x slower. Small alloc-and-copy ops are 44x slower. A scheduling flag recovers 57% of the gap. A worker-thread drain recovers up to 92%. B300 confidential NVSwitch reaches 510 GB/s NVLink P2P in a CVM. The abstract does not mention TDISP or IDE.
- FACT: "Benchmarking CC on Blackwell GPUs" (arXiv 2608.26575, Aug 2026). With correct configuration, overhead is 1 to 3%. Stock stacks lose 30 to 40%. It has a per-host-op fixed cost and a per-NVLink-traffic cost for encrypted collectives. The abstract does not mention KV offload or prefix cache.
- SNIPPET: ETH Zurich IISWC 2025 (arXiv 2509.18886). H100 CC costs 4 to 8% on Llama2-7B. The cost shrinks as batch grows.
- INTERPRETATION: The numbers disagree (1 to 3% against 13 to 27%) because of driver, platform and flag differences. Steady-state decode is cheap. Anything that moves bytes over the CPU-GPU bridge (KV offload, restore, model swap) is expensive.
- Side channel, FACT: "Early Bird" (arXiv 2409.20002) recovers system prompts with about 89% accuracy at about 111 queries per token (SNIPPET for those numbers). "I Know What You Asked" (PromptPeek, NDSS 2025) uses scheduling order. "Auditing Prompt Caching" (ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.07776) detects global cache sharing in 7 API providers (SNIPPET).
- FACT: KVGov (arXiv 2608.09225, single author, Aug 2026) measured a cold/cached TTFT ratio of 0.22 on A100 vLLM, and 0.093 on Apple Metal llama.cpp. Its defense results are simulation only. Boundary salting "retains 93%" is an estimate.
- FACT (docs, SNIPPET via search): vLLM `cache_salt` isolates tenants but it is opt-in and seeds the chain root, so it removes all cross-tenant reuse.
- Substitution: FACT-level title/ID known, numbers SNIPPET. "Are You Getting What You Pay For?" (arXiv 2504.04715) shows benchmark checks fail to detect quantization. RUT (arXiv 2506.06975, ICLR 2026) is a rank-based test, one call per prompt. SVIP (arXiv 2410.22307): FNR below 5%, FPR below 3%. TOPLOC (arXiv 2501.16007, ICML 2025): claims 100% detection of model, prompt or precision change in the authors' tests, 258 bytes per 32 tokens. LOGIC (Inference.net, Nov 2025, blog only).

### Prior art summary
PipeLLM (pipelined encryption), Serialized Bridge (Blackwell bridge cost), Blackwell CC benchmark, CachePrune (arXiv 2605.23640, privacy-aware fine-grained KV sharing, SNIPPET), KVGov (simulated), TOPLOC, SVIP, RUT, model-substitution audit, a CC survey for agents (arXiv 2605.03213, SNIPPET).

### Remaining gap
No paper found that combines the three costs in one serving design: per-tenant cache isolation (hit-rate loss), encrypted KV offload or restore (bridge cost), and the choice of what to keep resident when both are priced.

### Verdict: PARTIAL (CC overhead is CROWDED; verification is CROWDED; the isolation-versus-reuse-versus-offload trade-off is OPEN)

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB, public data (feasible, no GPU):
  - Take public traces with block hashes (for example Mooncake traces; I did not open them this session, so check the format). Compute the share of prefix blocks reused across users. This is the maximum hit-rate loss from per-tenant salting. A tenant-id column may be missing, so define tenants by a hash of the first block or by a synthetic split. State the assumption.
- One AWS H100: INTERPRETATION: AWS does not appear to offer H100 CC mode. Azure and GCP do (not verified). A CC run on one H100 for about 3 hours may fit $20 but check the price first.
  - Measure KV offload and restore time with CC on and off, for 8K to 128K contexts, against re-prefill time. Output: break-even context length at which restore beats recompute under CC.
  - Kill criterion: if restore under CC is still faster than recompute at 32K, the offload angle is weak.

## P7. "The server holds no session state; the client resends the conversation."

### Evidence the assumption is breaking
- FACT: OpenAI Responses API. `previous_response_id` and Conversations objects. Responses are kept 30 days by default, `store=false` disables it, and Conversation objects are not subject to the 30-day TTL. https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/conversation-state
- FACT: With `previous_response_id`, all earlier input tokens in the chain still count as billed input. The page does not mention KV cache or session lifetime. It also offers server-side compaction (`context_management`, `compact_threshold`) and a `/responses/compact` endpoint.
- SNIPPET: Gemini Interactions API uses `previous_interaction_id`, `store=true` by default, 55 days retention paid, 1 day free. Sources disagree on Beta versus GA.
- SNIPPET: Anthropic Managed Agents (beta header `managed-agents-2026-04-01`), Memory (public beta 2026-04-23), server-side compaction (beta header `compact-2026-01-12`, default threshold 150K). These come from third-party write-ups.
- SNIPPET: NVIDIA Dynamo session control on SGLang (v1.1.0 docs, marked experimental, FACT for status). Session KV sits outside the radix tree, cannot be evicted, and is freed by close or inactivity timeout. The search snippet said a default of 300 s. The page I opened gave no duration. Needs SGLang 0.5.11 and `--enable-streaming-session`.

### What breaks or becomes possible
- SNIPPET: OpenAI cookbook says cache utilization rose from 40% to 80% moving from Chat Completions to Responses. The API switch is confounded with chaining. A forum post (one anecdote) says the cache broke about 75% of the time with `previous_response_id` on o4-mini.
- INTERPRETATION: The server now sees session start, turn gaps, and the full history. It can pin KV for a chosen time, prefetch before the next turn, and route to one worker. The public docs describe none of this. Public stateful APIs expose state as an ID, not as a KV lifetime contract.
- INTERPRETATION: The durable state is the token log. The KV cache is soft state that can be rebuilt by re-prefill. Failover then has one question: restore or recompute. Durability is cheap for tokens and costly for KV.
- INTERPRETATION: Edits to history (compaction, pruning) break the prefix. FACT-level titles, SNIPPET numbers: CliffCompaction (arXiv 2609.26779) says any context change forces re-prefill and uncached tokens cost about 5 to 6x cached ones. TokenPilot (arXiv 2606.17016) targets cache-stable compaction.

### Remaining gap
No paper treats the public stateful API itself as the contract and derives retention time, restore-or-recompute and failover policy from the idle-gap distribution the server now sees, for human chat and for agent traffic together.

### Verdict: PARTIAL (agent KV retention is CROWDED; the human-chat idle-gap policy and the durability/failover cost model are OPEN)

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB, public data (feasible):
  - Take a public chat log with timestamps (WildChat is a candidate; I did not open it, so confirm timestamps and licence). Plot the inter-turn gap distribution and the per-turn context growth.
  - Compute, per session, the HBM-seconds cost to hold KV against the re-prefill cost at a published price (INTERPRETATION: use a cached/uncached price ratio of 5 to 6x from CliffCompaction as a proxy). Output: the share of turns where holding beats recompute, as a function of TTL.
  - Use TraceLab tool-gap stats (18 s mean, heavy tail) for the agent side.
- One AWS H100, under $20 (feasible):
  - Measure, for 8K to 128K contexts, re-prefill time against CPU-to-GPU KV reload on an 8B or 32B model. Output: break-even gap for hold, reload, recompute. Kill criterion: if recompute is within 20% of reload at all lengths, the durability question is moot.
