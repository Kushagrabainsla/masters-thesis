## P2. "An AI datacenter can draw full power at any time"

### Evidence that the assumption is breaking
- FACT: Emerald Conductor ran on a 256-GPU cluster in Phoenix (Oracle, NVIDIA, SRP, EPRI DCFlex). It cut cluster power by 25% for 3 hours during a peak event. Source: arXiv 2507.00909 (Jul 2025).
- FACT: The same paper reports 33 experiments over 212 jobs with zero SLA violations. SRP and APS events held 25% for 3 hours. A CAISO replay did 15% and then a further 10%.
- FACT: Utility rules asked for a 15 minute ramp down and up, and no snap-back above baseline.
- FACT: A 185-day trace of 155,410 GPUs shows the eligible flexible share is small. Immediate eligible curtailment is 3.55 MW, which is 12.1% of workload power and 6.35% of median facility power (55.8 MW). Source: arXiv 2609.05406 (Sep 2026). Scalar flexibility models overstate 4-hour relief by 25%.
- SNIPPET: Acun et al. (BU, e-Energy 2026) report 18% to 55% flexibility relative to average power for training plus inference in real demand response programs. I could not read the PDF text.
- SNIPPET: Google signed demand response agreements for ML workloads with Indiana Michigan Power and TVA (Aug 2025). Earlier test with Omaha Public Power District: three emergency events in 2024.
- SNIPPET: PJM filed at FERC (Docket ER26-3515, Aug 2026). Large loads of 50 MW or more that enter service after 1 Jun 2027 and lack new qualifying capacity face curtailment before PJM calls paid demand response. A market monitor criticizes missing curtailment priority rules.
- SNIPPET: Texas SB 6 lets utilities curtail large loads that connect after 31 Dec 2025 during firm load shed. ERCOT offers a Provisional Controllable Load option. Aurora modeling gives 50 to 241 curtailment hours per year at three sites.
- SNIPPET: Emerald AI and NVIDIA build a 100 MW flexible site in Manassas, Virginia. The AI Energy Management Alliance (NVIDIA, Google, Emerald) launched in Sep 2026.
- INTERPRETATION: Interconnection rules now sell speed of connection in exchange for curtailment rights. This turns power flexibility into a contract term, so software must meet it.

### What breaks
- FACT: Strict reading of the Emerald paper: training jobs are mostly Flex 3 (up to 50% throughput loss accepted). Most LLaMA inference is Flex 0 (no reduction). MPT pretraining was the most power-sensitive job at mid-range caps. Checkpoint cost is called negligible but not measured.
- FACT: PowerSlider reports that baselines reach only 47.6% online goodput at a 30% cap, and tail latency of 2.3x to 6x (up to 12x) of nominal. In a CAISO emergency replay at 0.41x nominal power, every baseline falls below 7% goodput.
- SNIPPET: inference-fleet-sim notes that capping batch size under a 10% to 30% cut for 15 to 60 minutes can break the TTFT SLO through queueing.
- FACT: Microsoft, OpenAI and NVIDIA (arXiv 2508.14318, Aug 2025) show that synchronous training swings power between compute and communication phases. The swing spectrum can match grid resonance. The abstract gives no MW or Hz numbers.
- INTERPRETATION: Removing data-parallel replicas changes the global batch size. The learning rate and the data order then need a rule. No source I found measures this.

### Gap and verdict
- Gap: nobody compares checkpoint-and-pause, DVFS and replica dropping on one grid trace for synchronous training, with loss curves and ramp-rate limits measured.
- Verdict: PARTIAL for training, CROWDED for inference. Elasticity systems and Emerald cover the mechanics. The comparison with convergence evidence is thin. Risk is high that Emerald or the Coskun group publishes it next (same authors wrote 2609.11542).

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB: cannot measure GPU power caps. It can replay the public trace statistics from 2609.05406 against a simple model of ramp limits and checkpoint cost. That tests only the model, not hardware.
- One AWS H100: possible, about $20. Run a small data-parallel job on one H100 (an 8-GPU p5 node costs too much; a single-GPU p5.4xlarge price must be checked, INTERPRETATION). Measure tokens per second and joules per token at power caps of 100%, 80%, 70% and 60%. Add the checkpoint save and restore time. Compare to a pause. One GPU cannot test replica dropping, so use gradient accumulation to emulate fewer replicas and measure loss versus tokens.

## P3. "Users of a GPU cluster are humans who submit a few large jobs"

### Evidence
- FACT: Karpathy autoresearch runs 5 minute experiments on one H100, about 12 per hour.
- FACT: SkyPilot scaled it to 16 GPUs (13 H100, 3 H200) on Kubernetes. The agent submitted about 910 experiments in 8 hours (about 700 valid), around 90 per hour. It reached the same best val_bpb 9x sooner than a simulated sequential run (8 h against 72 h). Total cost was under $300.
- FACT: Each experiment takes about 5 minutes of training plus about 1 minute of setup and agent thinking. So the non-training share is about 1/6 of wall time, or 17%. The setup block runs once per cluster.
- FACT: After about 700 experiments gains fell below 0.0001 per experiment. This is waste from low-value runs.
- FACT: The agent found that H200 results differ from H100 by about 0.005 val_bpb for the same config. This is a hidden confound when jobs hop across hardware.
- FACT: arXiv 2603.15916 (Mar 2026): two agents ran 10,469 experiments in 27 days. Architecture choices explain 94% of variance. Its orchestrator deduplicates proposals.
- FACT: AlphaLab (arXiv 2604.08590, Mar 2026) runs a Strategist/Worker loop on GPUs. The search snippet says its dispatcher uses Slurm and reassigns freed GPUs by priority (SNIPPET for the dispatcher detail).
- SNIPPET: Meta FAIR preference models rank candidate experiments before the GPU runs (arXiv 2608.13940). They match the unguided agent's 24 h result in about 15 h with under two thirds of the budget.

### What breaks
- Startup: 17% overhead at SkyPilot's 5 minute scale. For runs of 30 to 60 seconds the share would be much larger (INTERPRETATION).
- Redundancy and plateau: 210 of 910 submissions came after the plateau (FACT, derived from the phase table).
- Fairness: no source measures how an agent that issues 90 jobs per hour starves human users. INTERPRETATION only.
- Cold start numbers for GPU containers: MQFQ-Sticky reports up to 75x latency from cold starts and over 1.5 s to attach the GPU (SNIPPET).
- Checkpoint-restore snapshots: NVIDIA Dynamo snapshot lists about 1 minute cold against about 10 seconds restored for inference workers (SNIPPET). Doubleword's SGLang restore took 32 s (SNIPPET).

### Gap and verdict
- Gap: no scheduler treats an experiment-writing agent as a tenant, with config-hash duplicate detection, cross-job ASHA stopping, fork-from-parent checkpoints, warm GPU workers and per-tenant fairness in one system.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. Every part exists alone. The integration and its measured waste are open. Risk: SkyPilot or an agent lab can add this as a feature, because the pieces are simple.

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB: yes. Offline analysis only. Take the result logs of public autoresearch runs (check that they exist) and measure the near-duplicate rate (same config hash or small edit distance), the plateau share, and the 17% setup share. Also run a tiny training job on the M4 to time import and compile cost against train time.
- One AWS H100, under $20 (about 5 to 8 hours): yes. Run 100 agent-style short jobs. Compare cold container, warm Python process, and CRIU/cuda-checkpoint snapshot start. Measure seconds of startup per job and GPU idle share. Add a fork-from-parent variant that reuses the first N steps of near-duplicate runs, and measure saved GPU minutes.

## P4. "The model version is fixed for the whole lifetime of a session"

### Evidence
- FACT: Cursor Tab online RL needs 1.5 to 2 hours to roll out a checkpoint and collect the data for the next step, and ships "frequently throughout the day". The new model made 21% fewer suggestions with a 28% higher accept rate. Source: Cursor blog (Sep 2025).
- FACT: vLLM async RL docs: requests paused with mode "keep" produce old-weight tokens before the pause and new-weight tokens after resume.
- FACT: PipelineRL (arXiv 2509.19128, Sep 2025) updates weights in flight with minimal interruption on 128 H100s, about 2x faster learning. The abstract does not say how the KV cache is treated.
- SNIPPET: A 2026 paper states a single long trajectory spans several weight versions, and splits staleness into intra-trajectory and inter-trajectory parts (arXiv 2607.22186).
- SNIPPET: Provider-side silent change under a fixed model id is documented only by blogs. The Stanford/Berkeley study (2023) reports GPT-4 prime identification falling from 84% to 51% between March and June 2023. I found no primary data on how often weights change under a fixed id. The brief asks for this number and I do not have it.
- INTERPRETATION: Long agent sessions of hours to days now cross rollout cycles of 1.5 to 2 hours.

### What breaks
- FACT: With clear_cache=True every in-flight request re-prefills under new weights. With clear_cache=False, "some tokens in context may still reflect the old weights".
- FACT (PSRL docs): a 10-turn trajectory with 1k tokens per turn needs about 55k prefill tokens without reuse against about 10k with reuse. A flush on every update removes this reuse for all in-flight sessions. PSRL says stale-weight KV causes accuracy loss but gives no figure.
- SNIPPET: verl clears local and Mooncake KV at each update. SGLang's update endpoint has a weight version label and flush_cache on by default.
- FACT (PSRL docs): version-tagged KV keeps old entries until eviction, so memory grows with each live version. No figure given.
- INTERPRETATION: A re-prefill storm costs roughly (live sessions) x (context tokens) of prefill per update. At a 2 hour cadence with 10k-token contexts the cost is small per update. It grows with 100k-token agent contexts and many sessions. This needs measurement.

### Gap and verdict
- Gap: no system keeps several weight versions resident with reference counts for serving sessions (MVCC), and none tests approximate KV reuse between successive checkpoints of the same model.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. Delta weight sync is CROWDED. Version-tagged KV exists only inside RL stacks. Serving-side session pinning and cross-version KV reuse are OPEN. Risk: DroidSpeak authors could extend to checkpoints with little work. The question "does a sparse weight delta give nearly identical KV" has a quick test.

### Premise check
- Apple M4 16 GB: yes. Use public intermediate checkpoints (Pythia or OLMo revisions, INTERPRETATION that both publish them) at a 160M to 1B size. Feed 2k to 8k token prompts. Compare KV tensors per layer between adjacent checkpoints (cosine, relative error). Then reuse checkpoint A's KV under checkpoint B and measure perplexity or answer change. Also count the fraction of changed weight elements between checkpoints. This tests whether DroidSpeak-style layer recompute can be small for adjacent versions.
- One AWS H100, under $20: yes. With vLLM, run 64 sessions of 32k tokens, then swap weights. Measure the re-prefill time and TTFT spike for clear_cache=True against version-tagged KV. Measure the extra HBM per resident version for a 7B or 8B model (about 15 GB each in bf16, INTERPRETATION).

## Overall ranking for the thesis (INTERPRETATION)
1. P4 cross-version KV reuse plus MVCC weights: cheapest decisive test, fits the PagedAttention-style mechanism-transfer rule.
2. P3 agent-tenant scheduler: clear waste numbers exist, but the contribution risks being a systems integration with no new mechanism.
3. P2 training under demand response: strong evidence of demand, but the group at BU and Emerald publishes fast and holds field data. Needs hardware for a fair test.
